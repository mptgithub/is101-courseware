Why can AI give different answers to the same question?

AI doesn't look up a single correct answer. It predicts the most likely one — and "likely" isn't "certain." In this lab you'll see how that works, play with it, and learn to think critically about AI output.

Idea 1 Generative AI is probabilistic

A large language model doesn't "know" the one right next word. Every time it writes, it's ranking many possible next words by how likely each one is to come next — then picking one. That's what probabilistic means: the output is drawn from a distribution of possibilities, not looked up from a fact table.

    Idea 2 Generative AI is non-deterministic

    Because the model is sampling from a probability distribution instead of running a fixed lookup, asking the exact same question twice can produce two different answers. That's what non-deterministic means: same input, not-guaranteed-same output.

    Idea 3 How this affects your prompts

    You can't make AI fully deterministic, but you can narrow its options. A vague prompt leaves the model choosing from a wide, flat spread of possibilities — lots of variation. A specific prompt (format, constraints, examples) collapses that spread onto a few strong candidates — much more consistent, though still not identical every time.

      Check Quick self-check

      Not graded — just a gut check before you move on. Pick True or False for each statement.

      1. If you ask an AI the same question twice, you should expect a word-for-word identical answer both times.

      False. Because generation is non-deterministic, the exact wording can change from one attempt to the next — even though the underlying idea is often similar.

      2. An AI chooses its response by looking up the single correct answer in a database.

      False. It's predicting the most likely next words based on patterns it learned — there's no single stored "correct answer" it's retrieving.

      3. Giving an AI more specific instructions can make its answers more consistent.

      True. Specific prompts narrow the probability spread the model is sampling from, so outputs cluster more tightly — though "more consistent" still isn't "identical."
