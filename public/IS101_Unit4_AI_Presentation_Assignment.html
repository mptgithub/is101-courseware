AI-Powered Presentations: History of Writing Instruments

IS101 · Unit 4 · AI Presentation Tools Assignment

← Back to Courseware

Objective

AI tools can now generate an entire slide deck from a single sentence — but how good is "good enough"? In this assignment, you will use the exact same prompt in three different AI presentation tools, compare what each one produces, and decide which result is actually the best fit for a real presentation.

You are not just building slides — you are evaluating AI tools as a creation method: their strengths, their limits, and how much human editing they still require.

The Prompt

Use this prompt, word-for-word, as your starting point in each tool:

Prompt

"Create a 10 slide deck about the history and progress of writing instruments starting from the quill pen to the current day ball point pens. Include graphics and the year of each type of invention."

Important: You must use an AI tool that actually lets you submit this prompt yourself — a tool where you type or paste the prompt in and the AI generates the deck. Manually building slides yourself and calling it "AI-assisted" does not meet the assignment requirement.
Step 1

Generate the Deck in Three AI Tools

Choose any three AI tools that can turn a text prompt into a slide deck. A few suggestions (in no particular order of preference):

OpenAI ChatGPT

General-purpose AI chat with slide/deck generation capability

Google Gemini AI

Google's AI assistant, integrated with Slides

Gamma.app

AI-native presentation and document builder

invideo.ai

AI video and presentation generation tool

popai.pro

AI presentation and design assistant

You are welcome to use any three AI tools you like, from this list or elsewhere, as long as each one lets you submit the prompt directly.

Run the exact prompt above in each of your three chosen tools, and save or export each result (as a PowerPoint file if the tool supports it, or as a set of slide screenshots/PDF if it does not). You will need all three outputs to complete your comparison in Step 3.

Step 2

Refine if You Want To (Optional)

The original prompt is intentionally short. Part of this assignment is judging whether that was enough on its own.

If you feel a tool's first output is missing something — more accurate dates, better graphics, more detail per slide — you're welcome to send that tool additional follow-up prompts to improve the result. This is optional, and it's fine if you decide the first prompt alone was sufficient for some or all of your three tools.

Keep track of any follow-up prompts you use — you'll need to describe them in your analysis in Step 3, including which tool you used them on and what changed.
Step 3

Select the Best, and Write Your Analysis

Once you have all three outputs, pick the one deck you feel is the best overall quality based on the original prompt. That deck (refined further if you'd like) is your final 10-slide submission.

Then write up your analysis covering the following:

1. Compare and Contrast the Three Decks

How did the three outputs differ? Consider content accuracy (did they get the history and years right?), visual design, use of graphics, slide structure, and how closely each one followed the prompt's requirements (10 slides, graphics, a year for each invention).

2. Why You Preferred One Over the Others

Explain, with specific examples, why you selected this deck as the best. What did it get right that the other two didn't?

3. Was the Prompt Sufficient?

Did the single prompt produce a complete, accurate, usable presentation on its own — or did you need (or want) to use additional prompts to get a better result? If you used follow-up prompts, describe what you asked for and on which tool.

4. Your Experience with Each Tool

For each of the three tools, describe your experience: ease of use, quality of the output, how easy it was to modify or regenerate slides, and the accuracy of the historical information it produced. The table below is a good way to organize this before you write it up:

5. Did You Need PowerPoint to Finish the Job?

Was the AI tool's output good enough to submit as-is (or exported directly to .pptx), or did you need to open it in PowerPoint to fix formatting, correct facts, or clean up the design? Be specific about what you had to change, if anything.

What to Submit

  • Your final 10-slide PowerPoint deck (.pptx) — the best of your three AI-generated outputs, refined further if you chose to
  • Your written analysis covering all five points in Step 3 above

Tips for Success

  • Use the exact prompt first in all three tools before customizing anything — that's what makes the comparison fair.
  • Check the count — confirm each deck actually has 10 slides, includes graphics, and shows a year for each writing instrument mentioned.
  • Fact-check the history — AI tools sometimes get dates or inventors wrong. Note any inaccuracies you catch; that's valuable material for your accuracy write-up.
  • Export or screenshot early — some free AI tools limit exports or require a paid plan after your first generation, so save your work as soon as it's ready.
  • Be specific in your comparison — "Tool A was better" is weaker than "Tool A included a graphic and correct year for all 10 slides, while Tool B skipped years on 3 slides and Tool C's graphics didn't match the writing instruments described."
