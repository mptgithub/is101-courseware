AI vs. You: Can AI Complete a Real Excel Project?

IS101 · Unit 4 · AI Excel Accuracy Assignment

← Back to Courseware

Objective

Back in Unit 3, you built the Variation 1 Excel project yourself — writing formulas like SUM, IF, nested IF, COUNTIF, VLOOKUP, and IFERROR by hand, and your workbook was graded automatically against exact criteria.

This time, you're handing the same job to an AI tool. You'll give a general-purpose AI the exact same starting materials you had — the blank starter file and the instructions — and see whether it can independently produce a correct, complete workbook. Then you'll compare its work to your own, formula by formula, to judge how well it actually did.

Step 1

Re-Download Your Original Materials

Go to Excel Projects, find the Variation 1 card under Variation Projects, and download both files again — the same ones you used the first time:

Use a fresh copy of the starter file — not your completed submission. The AI tool should be starting from the same blank point you did.
Step 2

Let an AI Tool Complete the Project

Open any AI tool that can accept file uploads and work with them directly — for example ChatGPT, Gemini, or Copilot. Upload both files (the starter workbook and the instructions PDF) and ask it to complete the project. A prompt like this works well:

Prompt

"Read the attached instructions PDF and complete every task in the attached Excel starter file according to those instructions. Return the completed workbook."

Download the completed workbook the AI tool gives you back.

Step 3

Compare the Two Results — Visually, By Hand

You cannot run the AI's file through the automated grader. The grader checks for specific formula structures and values tied to your original completed submission, so it isn't a valid or fair way to score a workbook built independently by an AI tool. This comparison has to be done by you, visually.

Open both workbooks side by side — the AI's completed file, and your own originally-graded Variation 1 submission. Use the instructions PDF as your checklist, and go task by task, not just glancing at whether the final numbers look plausible:

Check What to look for
The formula itselfClick each relevant cell and read the actual formula the AI wrote — not just the displayed value. Did it use the correct function (SUM, IF, nested IF, COUNTIF, VLOOKUP, IFERROR)?
Cell referencesAre the ranges and lookups pointing at the right cells, or did it reference the wrong row/column?
Real data vs. invented dataDid the AI compute values from the source data, or did it "hallucinate" plausible-looking numbers instead of calculating them?
FormattingNumber formats, headers, and any formatting instructions from the PDF — did it follow them?
CompletenessDid it attempt all 26 tasks, or skip/ignore any?
Step 4

Write Your Analysis

1. The Verdict

Did your AI tool complete the assignment completely, partially, or not at all? Give a task-by-task breakdown — which of the 26 tasks did it get fully correct, which were partially right, and which were wrong or missing entirely?

2. What Kind of Mistakes Did It Make (If Any)?

Were the errors formula-logic mistakes, wrong cell references, misread instructions, formatting misses, or invented data? Give specific cell/task examples.

3. Different Path, Same Answer?

Did the AI ever solve a task with a different (but still valid) formula than the one you used, and still land on the correct result? Note any places its approach differed from yours even when the outcome was right.

4. Would You Trust It?

Based on what you saw, would you trust this AI tool to complete real spreadsheet work unsupervised — for a job, for example? Why or why not?

What to Submit

  • The AI-completed Excel workbook, exactly as the AI tool returned it (no corrections from you)
  • Your written analysis covering all four points in Step 4 above

Tips for Success

  • Your originally-graded submission is your answer key — it already passed the grader, so trust it as the correct reference when judging the AI's version.
  • Check formulas, not just numbers — an AI tool can land on the right number with the wrong (or even hardcoded) formula. Click into cells to see what's actually there.
  • Watch for confident wrong answers — AI tools rarely say "I'm not sure." A wrong value will look just as polished as a correct one.
  • Screenshot mismatches as you find them — it'll make writing your task-by-task breakdown in Step 4 much easier.
  • Don't fix the AI's file before submitting it — we want to see exactly what it produced, mistakes included.
